01 — Projects

What got built

Five teams, twenty-four hours and one theme! Here's what the first-ever Hamilton Hacks produced: from climate tools to conservation apps, all built around the environment.

MoveOn

Garret Keyhani, Jalen Reese, Max Freishtat, Jack Stowell
We wanted to reduce waste by targeting a very relevant issue on campus: the constant waste of items as people move through their time as a student. Books, furniture, sports gear, etc.

GitHub
Air Quality Index (AQI) Comparison Dashboard

Sicong(Simon) Li, Yuxing(Stella) Zhang
A Streamlit-based web application that provides real-time Air Quality Index (AQI) data and health impact analysis for cities in China and the United States. User can compare air quality between locations and recognize the health implications of pollution levels.

GitHub
Disaster Prevention App

Ashkhan, Addis, Pan
It is about the environment, particularly climate change and disaster prevention. We built our project entirely in Python, using NASA and Google Maps API. We used NiceGUI, which allowed us to build our website entirely in Python.

GitHub
Prey/Predator Simulation

Geoffrey Wolf
It models the environment of Yellowstone national park. I used matplotlib, c++ and python. c++ did the calculation and python did the visual work.

GitHub
HamSpot

Rashid Mikidadi, Venakuro Issahaque, Neo Liu, Aakarshan Guragai
HamSpot helps students find the environment that works for them. Seeing live campus patterns nudges students to choose off-peak times — a small behavioural shift that compounds across thousands of daily decisions on campus.

GitHub
02 — About

More than just a hackathon

Hamilton Hacks is Hamilton College's first hackathon, bringing together students from all disciplines to tackle real-world problems through technology and creativity. Over 24 hours of building, learning, and collaborating, teams created projects that engage with environmental challenges. Whether a seasoned developer or a first-time builder, every participant brought something valuable to the table.

04 — FAQ

Good questions

Hamilton Hacks is Hamilton College's first hackathon where students from all backgrounds spend 24 hours building creative tech projects. No experience? No problem — we welcome all beginners!
Any currently enrolled Hamilton College student is eligible to participate. We welcome students of all majors and skill levels — you don't need to be a CS major to hack!
Not at all! Teams benefit from diverse skills — design, writing, research, and business thinking are just as valuable as coding. Workshops throughout the event will help beginners get up to speed.
Yes! Hamilton Hacks is completely free. We'll provide meals, snacks, and caffeine throughout the event.
Bring your laptop, charger, any hardware you plan to use, and something to sleep on if staying overnight. A water bottle and a positive attitude are highly recommended.
Anything that connects to the Environment theme! Web apps, mobile apps, hardware, data visualizations — if you can build it in 24 hours, it's fair game.
